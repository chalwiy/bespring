STPP vs SHMP: How Industrial Buyers Should Compare the Two Phosphates

Compare STPP and SHMP by chemical identity, function, grade, specification, physical form and supplier-qualification requirements.

STPP and SHMP are both sodium phosphates, but they are not interchangeable names for the same material. A sound purchase starts with the required chemistry, function, grade and specification—not the lowest price per tonne.

What is the core difference?

Sodium tripolyphosphate (STPP) is a condensed phosphate with the formula Na5P3O10. Sodium hexametaphosphate (SHMP) is a commercial glassy polyphosphate commonly represented by (NaPO3)n. Their chain structure, hydration behavior and sequestration performance differ, so selection must follow the process requirement.

Qualification points buyers should compare

Grade and governing specification

Confirm food, technical or another agreed grade and the exact referenced standard. The chemical name alone does not establish suitability for food contact, drinking water or any regulated use.

Critical analytical limits

Compare assay or total phosphate basis, pH, insoluble matter, iron, heavy metals and other limits relevant to the use. For STPP, phase composition or hydration behavior may matter. For SHMP, average chain characteristics and solution behavior can be important.

Physical form and handling

Powder, granular form, bulk density and particle size affect dissolution, dust and feeding. Ask for the current SDS and evaluate storage conditions, moisture protection and material compatibility.

What to include in an RFQ

  • Full product name and grade
  • Target standard and specification
  • Required physical form and particle-size range
  • Quantity, packing and pallet requirements
  • Destination market, port and required documents

Final selection should be validated by the buyer's technical, quality and regulatory teams against the intended system.

For food-ingredient sourcing, review food-grade STPP and food-grade SHMP. For mineral-processing requirements, see SHMP for mining and mineral processing. Qualify the exact grade for your intended use.
