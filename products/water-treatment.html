Procurement guidance

Match the chemistry to system conditions

Water-treatment chemical sourcing should define active concentration, basicity or charge characteristics where relevant, impurity limits, physical form and intended water-system category.

Products with the same common name may differ in concentration, basicity, density or metal content. Jar testing, microbiological control studies or corrosion-program review may be required before commercial adoption.

Important: Biocides, oxidizers, chlorine products and amines require product-specific risk assessment. Never infer dosing, compatibility or potable-water approval from a portfolio listing.

01

Water-system context

Identify municipal, industrial, cooling, boiler, process or wastewater service and provide relevant water-analysis data.

02

Product parameters

Confirm active concentration, density, pH, basicity, charge density or other chemistry-specific parameters.

03

Regulatory route

State whether the material may contact potable water, food operations or regulated discharge streams.

04

Logistics & storage

Confirm bulk, IBC, drum or bag supply, destination, storage compatibility and dangerous-goods requirements.

Buyer documentation

Documents to align before approval

Availability and scope depend on the exact product and source offered. Request current, applicable documents during qualification.

SPEC

Specification / TDS

Product identity, grade, reference limits and technical characteristics for commercial review.

SDS

Safety Data Sheet

Classification, handling, storage, transport and workplace-control information for the offered material.

COA

Certificate of Analysis

Representative or batch-specific test results compared with the mutually agreed specification.

CERT

Applicable Certificates

Current quality, product or site documents where relevant to the offered supply and destination.
