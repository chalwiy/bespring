Product identity

What is food grade monocalcium phosphate monohydrate?

Monocalcium phosphate monohydrate (MCP or MCPM), standardized as calcium dihydrogen phosphate, is the hydrated acidic calcium phosphate Ca(H2PO4)2·H2O. JECFA identifies the monohydrate as CAS 10031-30-8 under INS 341(i).

JECFA lists buffering, firming, sequestrant, leavening, dough-conditioning, texturizing, yeast-food and nutrient functions. In chemical leavening, water brings MCP and bicarbonate into reaction, releasing carbon dioxide; production success depends on the exact neutralizing and gas-release profile.

Bakery performance—not a commodity assay alone

Neutralizing value and reaction timing

Neutralizing value (NV) expresses the mass of sodium bicarbonate neutralized by 100 parts of leavening acid. An industry reference commonly gives MCP monohydrate NV around 80, but the contractual value and method must come from the exact offered grade.

Acid–bicarbonate balance

Use the certified NV to calculate a starting ratio. Then adjust only after measuring finished-product pH, taste, color and residual alkalinity/acidity.

Early gas release

Uncoated MCP monohydrate is generally screened as a fast-acting acid. Track gas loss during mixing, transfer and bench hold before the structure sets.

Blended acid systems

Where oven-stage gas is needed, MCP can be evaluated with a slower leavening acid. Calculate each acid from its own NV and confirm total reaction by bake testing.

Grade variables

Particle size, coating/treatment, moisture, temperature, water availability, batter viscosity and ingredient order can shift the apparent reaction profile.

Do not publish a universal dosageNV is a stoichiometric starting tool, not a finished formula. Flour, cocoa, dairy solids, acids, salts and process conditions also consume or contribute acidity and change gas retention.

Application-specific screening

Where food grade MCP is evaluated

These are technical screening paths, not universal permissions or guaranteed outcomes.

Baking powder and dry premixes

Set NV, gas-release split, particle compatibility, segregation, humidity protection and shelf reaction. Test after the intended packaging and storage period.

Cakes, pancakes and short-hold batters

Evaluate early aeration, bench time, specific volume, cell structure, symmetry, crumb, color, pH and flavor under the actual mix and bake curve.

Cookies, biscuits and crackers

Measure spread, surface, color, texture and residual taste. Reaction balance can affect browning and dough handling as well as lift.

Mineral and other food systems

Where used for nutrient, buffering or firming functions, calculate calcium and phosphorus from certified composition and test solubility, sensory and complete-formula labeling.

Bench-to-line approval

How to qualify MCP in a bakery system

  1. 1

    Lock the identity

    Require food-grade monohydrate, CAS 10031-30-8, source standard, label, signed specification and lot traceability.

  2. 2

    Balance from certified NV

    Calculate the starting bicarbonate ratio from the offered grade, while accounting for other formula acids and alkaline ingredients.

  3. 3

    Measure reaction timing

    Use matched water, temperature, mixer and hold time to track early and total gas release—not only final assay.

  4. 4

    Run product trials

    Compare volume, cells, symmetry, spread, color, pH, texture and taste against the approved benchmark.

  5. 5

    Stress the dry blend

    Test humidity, packaging, storage time, segregation and premature acid–bicarbonate reaction.

  6. 6

    Set receiving controls

    Agree identity, Ca assay, impurities, NV/reaction, particle distribution and COA fields with methods and change control.

Storage and handling

Protect hygroscopic MCP and premixes

  • Exclude moistureKeep approved bags sealed, dry and protected from puncture, contamination and humid air.
  • Use source conditionsThe supplied guidance says cool, dry, ventilated storage below 30°C; confirm shelf life and limits in the current specification.
  • Premix stabilityControl humidity and packaging after blending with bicarbonate to reduce premature reaction and activity loss.

Buyer questions

Food grade MCP FAQ

Is this page for monohydrate or anhydrous MCP?

This URL now primarily covers food grade monocalcium phosphate monohydrate, CAS 10031-30-8. Bespring has a separate page for anhydrous MCP, CAS 7758-23-8, to prevent hydration-form and search-intent confusion.

Is MCP always a fast-acting leavening acid?

Uncoated MCP monohydrate is generally screened as fast acting, but the actual profile depends on grade, particle size, treatment/coating, moisture and process. Commercial coated anhydrous MCP can be designed for slower release, so request reaction data.

What is neutralizing value?

NV is the parts by mass of sodium bicarbonate neutralized by 100 parts of leavening acid. An industry reference commonly lists about 80 for MCP monohydrate, but purchasing and formulation must use the certified value for the offered grade.

Does the supplied specification meet JECFA?

Not as written. The supplied lead limit is 5 mg/kg versus JECFA's 4 mg/kg maximum, loss-test labels conflict and several methods are undefined. A corrected source-specific specification is required.

What should an MCP RFQ include?

State food-grade monohydrate, CAS 10031-30-8, target standard, NV and reaction profile, particle size, bakery application and process, quantity, 25 kg packing/pallet needs, destination, Incoterm and certificates.
