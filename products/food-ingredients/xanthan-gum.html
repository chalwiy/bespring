Product identity and function

What is food grade xanthan gum?

Xanthan gum is a high-molecular-weight polysaccharide obtained by pure-culture fermentation of a carbohydrate with Xanthomonas campestris, followed by alcohol recovery, drying and milling. The JECFA 2016 specification identifies INS 415, CAS 11138-66-2 and sodium, potassium or calcium salt forms. It is a polymer rather than a substance with one fixed small-molecule formula or molecular weight; the offered source and grade should be identified in the specification.

JECFA lists thickening, stabilizing, emulsifying and foaming functions. For food formulation, xanthan is commonly selected for aqueous-phase viscosity and suspension: shear-thinning means viscosity decreases as shear rate increases. Validate pouring, pumping, separation and sensory performance in the complete formula; a general function does not guarantee emulsion stability or preservation.

Particle engineering and hydration

80 mesh vs. 200 mesh xanthan gum

Mesh describes particle-size screening, not the full grade. It does not by itself prove viscosity, clarity, microbiological quality or easy dispersion.

Coarser option

80 mesh

Can be considered for general food processing where the validated addition method gives complete hydration.

  • Confirm the percentage passing the stated sieve—not only “≥80 mesh”
  • Measure hydration time under the customer's mixer and water chemistry
  • Check undissolved particles, final viscosity and batch repeatability
Finer option

200 mesh

Provides a finer powder, but finer particles can wet and hydrate rapidly at the surface, increasing lump risk when poorly dispersed.

  • Confirm the actual 200-mesh passing requirement
  • Assess dusting, feeding accuracy and agglomeration during addition
  • Request evidence for any instant or easy-dispersing designation
Separate grade concept

Instant / easy-dispersing

An easy-dispersing designation needs source-specific grade information and a defined performance test. Agglomerated and other engineered grades are distinct from sieve size; neither 80 nor 200 mesh alone establishes instant performance.

  • Ask for treatment/composition disclosure relevant to labeling
  • Set a dispersion and hydration test in the real process
  • Verify full viscosity development after a stated time

Grade boundary: transparent-solution, rapid-hydration, low-dust, low-microbial or salt-tolerant grades are separate performance claims. Request a source-specific grade and evidence before using those descriptions.

Application-specific rheology

How xanthan gum is evaluated in food

Select a grade for the food matrix and process below, then establish the use level through formulation trials and applicable rules. U.S. 21 CFR 172.695 permits specified food functions under good manufacturing practice where food standards of identity do not preclude use; it also sets identity, manufacturing and testing conditions. E415 / INS 415 identifies the additive, not permission for every food or market.

Sauces, dressings and fillings

Target suspension and cling at rest with pour or pump flow under shear. Test yield behavior, mouthfeel, oil separation, acid/salt effects and viscosity recovery after processing.

Beverages and formulated drinks

Screen particle suspension and phase stability without excessive ropiness. Record hydration order, clarity/turbidity, protein/mineral interactions, heat treatment and sediment over shelf life.

Gluten-free bakery and dough systems

Evaluate water binding and structure with starches, proteins and other gums. Measure dough handling, gas retention, bake loss, crumb texture and staling rather than assuming a universal replacement ratio.

Frozen and chilled foods

Assess water mobility, freeze-thaw stability, syneresis and texture through the intended temperature cycle. The stabilizer system and solids profile influence the result.

Why “high viscosity” is incompleteOne viscosity number measures one point under defined conditions. A useful approval also compares low- and high-shear behavior, time-dependent recovery, temperature and the actual food matrix.

Prevent incomplete hydration

Dispersion and processing trial

If a trial gives unexpectedly low viscosity, check dispersion, hydration and measurement conditions before concluding that the grade is unsuitable. Use a controlled comparison against your approved reference.

  1. 1

    Define the test medium

    Record batch size, water temperature and hardness, pH, salts, sugars, proteins, oils and other hydrocolloids.

  2. 2

    Create particle separation

    Add at the point of adequate agitation or pre-disperse by an approved dry blend/oil-slurry route. Avoid dumping powder onto a quiet surface.

  3. 3

    Allow controlled hydration

    Fix addition rate, mixer type, speed, time and ingredient order. High sugar or salt concentration can delay water access and full hydration.

  4. 4

    Measure the relevant curve

    Compare viscosity at defined shear rates, flow after pumping/pouring, suspension at rest and recovery after shear.

  5. 5

    Validate the process and shelf

    Repeat heat, homogenization, filling, freeze-thaw and storage conditions; track separation, syneresis, texture and sensory acceptance.

  6. 6

    Lock the receiving method

    Translate the successful trial into agreed mesh distribution, viscosity method, microbiological limits and batch acceptance criteria.

Buyer questions

Food grade xanthan gum FAQ

Does 200 mesh mean instant or easy-dispersing xanthan gum?

No. Mesh describes sieve size, while easy dispersion is a grade-specific performance claim. Ask for the passing percentage and a dispersion/hydration test under your addition conditions; finer powder can still lump if added too quickly or with insufficient mixing.

What is needed to compare xanthan gum viscosity specifications?

Match concentration, medium (water or KCl solution), hydration procedure and time, temperature, instrument, spindle, speed or shear rate, and reading time. One mPa·s equals one cP, but values measured under different conditions are not directly comparable.

Is the shear value the same as the temperature viscosity ratio?

No. A shear ratio compares viscosity at defined shear conditions; the supplier must state its calculation and readings. The temperature ratio in 21 CFR 172.695 compares viscosities at 75°F and 150°F. Do not use that ratio to interpret the commercial shear value of ≥6.50.

Does xanthan gum form a firm gel by itself?

In the JECFA identity test, xanthan with carob/locust bean gum forms a firm gel, while the 1% xanthan-only control does not under the same conditions. Formulation texture and interactions with other gums require a food-system trial.

Does the commercial table establish JECFA conformity?

No. Complete methods, identity, dried-basis carbon-dioxide assay, residual-solvent and E. coli evidence are required in addition to numerical limits. The listed lead limit is a general-use reference and does not establish suitability for infant formula. A batch COA must identify the lot, agreed specification and reported results.

Can xanthan gum replace guar, carrageenan or gellan at the same dosage?

Do not assume an equal-weight substitution. These hydrocolloids differ in hydration, flow and gel behavior. Compare the specific grades under matched pH, salt, solids, processing and storage conditions, then validate the target suspension, texture and sensory result.

What information should accompany a xanthan gum RFQ?

State food grade and destination standard, 80/200 mesh or dispersion requirement, viscosity target and method, application and process, microbiological limits, quantity, packaging, pallet preference, destination port, Incoterm and delivery needs. List required specification, COA, TDS, SDS and source-specific certificates so availability and scope can be confirmed.
