Product overview

What is tetrapotassium pyrophosphate?

Tetrapotassium pyrophosphate (TKPP), also called tetrapotassium diphosphate or potassium pyrophosphate, is the potassium salt of pyrophosphoric acid. Its chemical formula is K4P2O7, its CAS number is 7320-34-5, and its international food additive designation is INS 450(v).

TKPP is hygroscopic and highly soluble in water. Its combination of alkalinity, phosphate functionality and metal-ion binding makes it useful in carefully designed food-processing systems. The appropriate dosage and legal use must be determined for the specific food category and destination market.

Functional roles

Why is TKPP used in food processing?

TKPP can perform several functions in a formulation. Actual performance depends on the complete phosphate system, raw materials, process conditions, dosage and target product attributes.

Function 01

Sequestration & process control

TKPP can bind selected metal ions and support process control in food systems where mineral interactions influence stability, appearance or equipment performance.

  • Metal-ion sequestration
  • Support for phosphate blend performance
  • Alkalinity and formulation adjustment
Function 02

Emulsification & texture support

In suitable protein, starch and dairy systems, TKPP may contribute to emulsification, water management, yield and finished-product texture.

  • Emulsifier and texturizer functionality
  • Water-retention support in selected systems
  • Process yield and consistency objectives

Buyer questions

Food grade TKPP FAQ

What is food grade TKPP used for?

TKPP is used in selected formulations as an emulsifier, sequestrant, texturizer and alkaline phosphate. Potential applications include processed meat, seafood, noodles, dairy systems, frozen desserts and canned foods, subject to trials and local regulations.

Is TKPP the same as tetrapotassium diphosphate?

Yes. Tetrapotassium pyrophosphate, tetrapotassium diphosphate and potassium pyrophosphate are commonly used names for K₄P₂O₇, CAS 7320-34-5 and INS 450(v).
