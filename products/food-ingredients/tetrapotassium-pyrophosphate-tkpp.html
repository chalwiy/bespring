Product overview

What is tetrapotassium pyrophosphate?

Tetrapotassium pyrophosphate (TKPP), also called tetrapotassium diphosphate or potassium pyrophosphate, is the potassium salt of pyrophosphoric acid. Its chemical formula is K4P2O7, its CAS number is 7320-34-5, and its international food additive designation is INS 450(v).

TKPP is hygroscopic and highly soluble in water. Its combination of alkalinity, phosphate functionality and metal-ion binding makes it useful in carefully designed food-processing systems. The appropriate dosage and legal use must be determined for the specific food category and destination market.

Potassium-based phosphate decision

When should a formulator evaluate TKPP?

TKPP is not simply a premium substitute for every sodium phosphate. Its value must be tested against the sodium target, potassium contribution, required pyrophosphate function, solution behavior and finished-food performance.

01

Sodium contribution

TKPP does not add sodium from the phosphate salt itself, so it may be screened against TSPP in sodium-reduction work. This does not make the finished food “low sodium”; calculate every sodium source and verify the legal claim criteria.

02

Potassium load

Pure anhydrous K4P2O7 is theoretically about 47.4% potassium by mass. Use the offered grade's certified composition for formulation and nutrition calculations.

03

Alkalinity and flavor

JECFA specifies pH 10.0–10.7 for a 1% solution. Excessive or poorly balanced use can change finished pH, flavor, color and protein texture.

04

Dissolution environment

Although TKPP is water-soluble, practical dissolution depends on concentration, temperature, water hardness, agitation, salt content and ingredient order. Check clarity and sediment in the actual brine or process water.

Claim boundary: replacing a sodium phosphate with TKPP can reduce sodium contributed by that ingredient, but nutritional claims require a calculation for the complete finished food and the destination market.

Functional roles

Why is TKPP used in food processing?

JECFA identifies TKPP as an emulsifier and texturizer. In applied systems, its alkaline pyrophosphate ion can also influence mineral–protein interactions; performance must be demonstrated in the complete formula.

Function 01

Mineral and pH management

Pyrophosphate can interact with polyvalent ions while TKPP raises solution pH. This may change protein hydration or emulsion behavior, but excessive alkalinity can create sensory and texture defects.

  • Metal-ion sequestration
  • Check water hardness and calcium load
  • Measure solution and finished-product pH
Function 02

Protein and emulsion functionality

In suitable meat or cheese systems, an alkaline phosphate system can support protein hydration and fat–water stabilization. Raw material, salt, shear, temperature and total phosphate determine the result.

  • Emulsifier and texturizer functionality
  • Protein extraction and hydration screening
  • Cook loss, purge and texture validation

Food industry uses

Common food grade TKPP applications

These are qualification pathways rather than universal permissions or dosage recommendations. The strongest technical fit is where potassium-based pyrophosphate functionality is deliberately required.

Meat and poultry brines

Compare TKPP within a phosphate system when sodium contribution, protein extraction, water binding or emulsion stability is under review. Record brine clarity, pickup, cook loss, chilled purge, texture and sensory quality.

Processed cheese systems

Screen calcium sequestration, casein hydration and fat–water emulsification. Control natural-cheese composition, calcium, moisture, pH, heating, shear and cooling before judging slice or spread texture.

Seafood processing

Where permitted, compare TKPP or a phosphate blend for solution behavior and retained yield. Report pickup after equal drainage, thaw/cook loss, texture and phosphate labeling rather than pickup alone.

Potassium-based phosphate blends

Use TKPP as one component only when its alkalinity and pyrophosphate functionality help meet a defined sodium, solubility or performance target. Validate blend compatibility and storage stability.

Avoid phosphate-name confusion

These materials are not interchangeable by acronym or equal weight. Compare the ion, phosphate chain, alkalinity, solubility, legal identity and validated function.

Buyer questions

Food grade TKPP FAQ

Why is TKPP evaluated instead of TSPP?

TKPP supplies potassium rather than the sodium supplied by TSPP and is water-soluble. It may fit a potassium-based or sodium-reduction strategy, but the complete sodium and potassium content, pH, functionality, flavor, cost and market rules must be assessed.

Is TKPP the same as tetrapotassium diphosphate?

Yes. Tetrapotassium pyrophosphate, tetrapotassium diphosphate and potassium pyrophosphate are commonly used names for K₄P₂O₇, CAS 7320-34-5 and INS 450(v).

Does TKPP make a finished food low sodium?

No. It avoids sodium from this phosphate salt, but sodium may still come from salt, proteins, flavors and other ingredients. Any low- or reduced-sodium claim must be calculated and validated under destination-market rules.

What must be checked before dissolving TKPP?

Define water temperature and hardness, target concentration, agitation, salt/mineral content and ingredient order. Record dissolution time, clarity, sediment and solution pH for the offered grade.

Does the supplied specification meet JECFA or EU limits?

Not as currently written: its fluoride limit is 30 mg/kg, while JECFA and the cited EU specification state 10 mg/kg. EU arsenic and lead limits are also tighter. Request a grade and batch COA matched to the required standard before approval.

What details are needed for an accurate TKPP quotation?

Provide the application, sodium and potassium targets, current phosphate benchmark, required pH/solubility/particle data, specification or compendium, quantity, packaging, destination, Incoterm and required documents.
