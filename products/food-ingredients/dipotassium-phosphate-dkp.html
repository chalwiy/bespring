Product identity and food grade

What is food grade dipotassium phosphate?

Dipotassium phosphate, also called dipotassium hydrogen phosphate, potassium phosphate dibasic or dibasic potassium phosphate, is K2HPO4. This page describes the anhydrous form: CAS 7758-11-4, molecular weight 174.18 g/mol and INS 340(ii) / E340(ii). The JECFA DKP monograph identifies this composition; hydrated forms require their own identity and specification.

JECFA describes colourless or white granules, crystals or masses and notes that DKP is deliquescent. It lists buffering agent, sequestrant and yeast-food functions. Confirm the offered powder or granular form and particle-size requirements rather than treating the reference images as a guaranteed size distribution. Food grade must be supported by the grade documents; the same chemical name does not establish suitability of a technical or fertilizer grade.

A buffer component—not a universal pH setting

How DKP behaves in a formulation

The phosphate acid/base balance, concentration, starting pH and other acids or salts determine the response of the complete formulation. The JECFA pH reference is 8.7–9.3 for a 1 in 100 solution: it is an identity/purity test, not the pH range guaranteed for a finished food.

01

Buffering capacity

DKP can resist pH movement within a formulation-dependent window. Measure titratable acidity and pH through processing and storage, not only after initial mixing.

02

Potassium contribution

DKP supplies both potassium and phosphate. Calculate elemental contribution from the certified assay and total formula; do not infer a nutrition claim from ingredient presence.

03

Mineral interaction

Phosphate can alter soluble calcium and other mineral equilibria. Hardness, fortification salts, proteins and heat can shift clarity, sediment and texture.

04

Dissolution and moisture

Because DKP is freely water-soluble and deliquescent, water quality, addition order, dry-blend humidity, liner integrity and caking control belong in qualification.

Design with a measured buffer curveScreen DKP alone and, where appropriate, with MKP or another permitted buffer. Record how much acid or base is required to move the real formulation across its critical pH range.

Protein beverages require system testing

DKP in dairy and plant-protein beverages

Buffer salts may support pH and mineral management during high-temperature processing, but DKP is not automatically beneficial in every protein beverage.

Protein source and history

Record MPC/MCC, whey or plant protein source, mineral composition, prior heat history and hydration. Poor protein hydration can cause graininess or settling independent of DKP.

Calcium and phosphate balance

Measure soluble and total minerals. DKP can change casein-mineral equilibria; excessive disruption may alter viscosity or release proteins and minerals from micelles.

Heat process and storage

Compare no-DKP and graded-DKP controls through HTST, UHT or retort conditions and shelf life. Track fouling, coagulation, sediment, viscosity, color and pH drift.

Finished sensory target

Check mineral taste, mouthfeel, color and label positioning together with stability. A technically stable drink can still fail sensory or nutrition requirements.

Evidence boundary: the 2024 Journal of Dairy Science study compared 0 and 0.15% DKP (wt/wt of the beverage) in fresh liquid MCC/MPC systems containing 7.5% milk protein. DKP increased soluble protein, calcium and phosphorus, while these beverages remained heat stable without it. The work included HTST and direct-steam-injection UHT treatments and refrigerated storage. This experimental level is not a recommended dose, a legal allowance or evidence of stability in every dairy or plant-protein drink.

Confirm lead control before grade approval

The commercial lead limit is ≤5 mg/kg, while the cited JECFA monograph specifies ≤4 mg/kg. This difference does not show that every batch fails, but the commercial limit alone cannot guarantee the JECFA requirement. Request a signed specification aligned with the selected standard, the method and actual batch COA. Complete the identity tests and remaining purity checks as well as the fields shown here.

Standards can differ. The original 2012 EU E340(ii) specification text gives P₂O₅ at 40.3–41.5% on an anhydrous basis and arsenic and lead at ≤1 mg/kg each. Those limits are narrower than the commercial P₂O₅, arsenic and lead references above. This is a version-specific comparison; confirm the currently applicable consolidated text and food-category conditions before EU supply approval.

FDA technical-effect context

The FDA inventory identifies DKP and lists emulsifier salt, nutrient supplement, pH-control, sequestrant and stabilizer/thickener effects.

Review the FDA entry

For US food use, 21 CFR 182.6285 recognizes dipotassium phosphate as safe when used in accordance with good manufacturing practice. This is not product approval or permission for every food and market. Technical functions, ingredient purity and permitted use must be assessed separately.

DKP vs. MKP, TKP and DSP

These orthophosphates are not equal-weight substitutes. Compare molar composition, solution pH, buffer need, potassium/sodium target and legal identity.

Buyer questions

Food grade DKP FAQ

Is this DKP anhydrous, and what is it used for in food?

This page describes anhydrous dipotassium phosphate, K₂HPO₄, CAS 7758-11-4, also called dipotassium hydrogen phosphate. JECFA lists buffering, sequestrant and yeast-food functions. The food standard, physical form and permitted use still require grade- and market-specific review; a hydrated or technical grade is not an automatic equivalent.

Does the commercial reference specification guarantee JECFA compliance?

No. Its lead limit is ≤5 mg/kg, compared with ≤4 mg/kg in the cited JECFA monograph. An individual batch may be lower, but a suitable signed specification, confirmed methods and batch COA are needed. Assay basis, pH concentration and drying duration also require confirmation.

Does a DKP solution pH of 8.7–9.3 set the final food pH?

No. JECFA gives that range for a 1 in 100 solution. The finished-food response depends on concentration, other acids and salts and the complete formulation. Measure pH and titratable acidity under the actual process and storage conditions.

Does DKP always improve protein-beverage heat stability?

No. Protein source, hydration, soluble minerals, calcium, pH, heat process and storage all matter. Compare the candidate grade with a no-DKP control. A result in one milk-protein system does not establish the same result in a plant-protein drink or a different shelf-life process.

What is the difference between DKP and MKP?

DKP is dibasic K₂HPO₄; MKP is the more acidic KH₂PO₄. Their potassium-to-phosphate ratio, solution pH and buffering behavior differ. Select by formulation and permitted use rather than substituting equal weights.

What information and documents are needed for a DKP quotation?

Provide anhydrous food grade, destination and food standard, assay basis, critical impurity limits, physical form, application and process, quantity, packaging and delivery terms. List required TDS, SDS, COA and declarations. Confirm commercial terms and any certificate scope in the actual offer.
