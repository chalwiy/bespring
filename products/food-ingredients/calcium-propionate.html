Product overview

What is food grade calcium propionate?

Calcium propionate, also called calcium propanoate, is the calcium salt of propionic acid: CAS 4075-81-4, formula C6H10CaO4, INS 282 and E282. The JECFA identity specification describes white crystals, powder or granules with at most a faint propionic-acid odour. Agree the actual particle form for your mixing and addition process.

Calcium propionate is used to inhibit susceptible spoilage moulds and rope-forming bacteria in appropriate bakery systems. Its effect depends on food pH, water activity, temperature, dose, distribution and the organism. The experimental study of bread-spoilage fungi shows why performance must be checked in the finished formulation; it does not establish a universal dose or pathogen-control claim.

The formula and 186.22 g/mol molar mass shown describe calcium propionate without water of crystallization. If a hydrated material is specified, confirm its hydrate form, corresponding molar mass and assay basis. A water result alone does not identify the crystal form.

From ingredient to shelf life

Design a bakery preservation trial around the full system

A useful calcium propionate trial separates preservative performance from process and packaging effects. Define a control, document the variables below and assess repeat batches under realistic storage and distribution conditions.

01

Formula environment

Record dough and finished-product pH, moisture and water activity. Propionate participates in a pH-dependent equilibrium with propionic acid; the undissociated acid fraction changes with pH.

02

Process performance

Record dose and its basis (for example, flour weight or finished-food weight), addition point, dissolution or dry-blending method, mixing, fermentation, proof time, bake profile, volume and sensory quality.

03

Hygiene & cooling

Control post-bake exposure, cooling time and contact surfaces. Recontamination can obscure the effect of a formulation change.

04

Package & distribution

Use the intended package and challenge realistic storage temperature, transport time and opening conditions across repeat batches.

Technical context: WHO/JECFA discussion of propionate acid–salt activity and primary research on pH, water activity, temperature and fungal response. Use these findings to choose trial variables, not to transfer a laboratory concentration directly to production.

Read the example results correctly

The values illustrate one supplied result set. Request the source COA and its lot/date identification for traceability. Keep the specification limits, actual batch results and requirements for any FCC material or blend separate.

Methods and reporting basis: The supplied assay basis and analytical methods are not stated. Confirm dried versus as-received assay; whether “Water” is a water determination or loss on drying; and the preparation basis of the 10% pH solution. The source label “Pb²⁺ (Heavy metals)” is ambiguous: obtain its test definition and a separate lead result before using it to assess a lead limit.

Compare commercial limits with the governing standard

The JECFA monograph specifies assay ≥98.0% on the dried basis, water-insoluble matter ≤0.3%, fluoride ≤30 mg/kg, lead ≤5 mg/kg and iron ≤50 mg/kg. It also specifies loss on drying ≤4% at 105°C for 2 hours. The commercial assay range of 99.0–100.5% can only be compared directly once its basis and method are confirmed; the table has no separate lead or iron result.

Fluoride and EU E282: The commercial limit of 0.003% equals 30 mg/kg (30 ppm by mass), matching the cited JECFA numeric limit. EU Regulation 966/2014 sets the E282 fluoride limit at 20 mg/kg, so the listed commercial limit is less stringent. The example result <0.003% does not establish ≤20 mg/kg. Request the complete EU specification and a sufficiently precise batch result when EU conformity is required.

Independent identity and use references

See the FAO/WHO JECFA calcium propionate monograph, U.S. eCFR 21 §184.1221 and NIH PubChem compound record. The 2025 official CFR text identifies calcium propionate as an antimicrobial agent for specified food categories under GMP and incorporates a named FCC edition. This is a regulatory provision, not a certification of Bespring’s product. Additive identity, purity requirements and finished-food permissions must be assessed separately.

Buyer questions

Calcium propionate FAQ

What is food grade calcium propionate used for?

It is used as a preservative, antimould and antirope ingredient. It is especially associated with bread and baked goods, and may also be used in other food categories where destination-market regulations permit.

How does calcium propionate help preserve bread?

It can inhibit susceptible spoilage moulds and rope-forming bacteria. Food pH affects the propionic-acid/propionate equilibrium; water activity, temperature, dose and distribution also affect results. Use finished-product trials to establish mould-free life and fermentation compatibility.

Does E282 mean the same thing as FCC compliance?

No. E282 and INS 282 identify calcium propionate; FCC is a specification compendium. A calcium propionate grade may need to meet both applicable market rules and a named FCC edition. Request the exact specification and supporting results rather than treating these labels as separate grades.

Is blended calcium propionate available?

Contact sales to discuss a blend for your formulation and addition method. Confirm its full composition, active content, carrier, labeling and acceptance criteria; the single-ingredient specification on this page does not describe a blend.

How does calcium propionate differ from sodium propionate?

They are salts of the same acid but have different cations and additive identifiers: calcium propionate is E282 / INS 282; sodium propionate is E281 / INS 281. Compare propionic-acid equivalent, calcium or sodium contribution, physical form and recipe compatibility. Replacing one with the other requires a recalculated formulation and validation, not an automatic equal-weight substitution.

What addition level should I use in bread?

There is no universal addition level for every bread. First establish the permitted use and calculation basis for the food category and market, then conduct controlled trials against the spoilage target while checking proof time, volume and flavour. State whether the amount is calculated on flour, dough or finished-food weight.

Are the example results a COA for my order?

No. They are a supplied reference result set. Request a COA identifying the actual shipment lot and methods, and compare it with your agreed specification. A result reported as less than a threshold may not be precise enough to establish conformity with a stricter limit.

Can I request a specification, COA, TDS or SDS?

Yes. Request the documents applicable to the exact standard or blended product being evaluated. A shipment COA should be checked against the agreed purchase specification.

What information is needed for an accurate quotation?

Provide the governing standard and edition, critical limits, powder or granular preference, single ingredient or blend, quantity, packaging, destination, food application, documents and delivery target.
