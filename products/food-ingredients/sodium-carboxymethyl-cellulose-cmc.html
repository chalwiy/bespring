Product overview

What is food grade sodium carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC)?

Sodium carboxymethyl cellulose is the sodium salt of a carboxymethyl ether of cellulose. It is also known as sodium CMC, Na-CMC or cellulose gum. When dispersed and properly hydrated in water, it forms a viscous colloidal solution that can modify texture and improve physical stability.

Food grade CMC is commonly specified by purity, viscosity, degree of substitution, pH, moisture and particle size. Viscosity is not a single universal number: the test concentration, temperature, hydration procedure and instrument settings all affect the reported result.

Identity note: CAS 9004-32-4 identifies the sodium salt described on this page. CAS 9000-11-7 identifies carboxymethyl cellulose more generally; buyers should keep the chemical name, salt form, CAS number and E466/INS 466 identity aligned across the specification, SDS and COA.

Functional performance

What does CMC do in food formulations?

The selected grade, use level and process determine the final result. Formulation trials should confirm performance in the buyer's actual system.

Flow & texture

Viscosity control

CMC can increase aqueous-phase viscosity and help formulators create a more consistent body, pour and mouthfeel.

  • Low to high viscosity grades available
  • Performance depends on concentration and hydration
  • Test method must accompany the target range
Physical stability

Stabilization & suspension

In suitable systems, CMC helps slow particle settling, reduce phase separation and maintain a more uniform product structure.

  • Supports suspended solids and uniformity
  • Can improve water binding in selected foods
  • Compatibility should be checked in the full formula

Reference and grade distinction

Sources: FAO JECFA Monographs 11 — Sodium Carboxymethyl Cellulose and Codex GSFA — INS 466. Food grade, detergent grade and other industrial grades are not interchangeable; each has its own purity, impurity, documentation and performance requirements.

Important: Food-additive approval, permitted uses, dosage and labeling are application- and market-specific. The US FDA food-substance inventory identifies sodium carboxymethyl cellulose under CAS 9004-32-4 and lists technical effects including stabilizer/thickener and texturizer; buyers must still confirm the regulation applicable to the intended formulation and destination market.

Buyer questions

Food grade CMC FAQ

What is food grade sodium carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC)?

Food grade CMC is sodium carboxymethyl cellulose, also called cellulose gum or E466. It is a cellulose-derived ingredient used primarily as a thickener, stabilizer and suspension agent in food processing.

What viscosity grades of food grade CMC are available?

Food grade CMC is supplied in multiple viscosity ranges, but a grade name or viscosity number is meaningful only with its complete test method. Send the solution concentration, temperature, hydration procedure, instrument, spindle, speed and target range so an appropriate candidate can be reviewed.

Can the viscosity and other CMC specifications be customized?

Yes. We can review requirements for viscosity, degree of substitution, purity, pH, moisture, particle size, bulk density and packaging. Send your specification and test methods so feasibility can be evaluated before the designated grade is confirmed for production.

How should I specify CMC viscosity?

State the solution concentration, test temperature, hydration procedure and time, viscometer model or spindle, rotational speed and target range. For example, a result measured at 1% solution cannot be compared directly with a 2% solution result.

Which CAS number applies to sodium CMC?

FAO/WHO JECFA and PubChem identify sodium carboxymethyl cellulose under CAS 9004-32-4. CAS 9000-11-7 refers to carboxymethyl cellulose without specifying the sodium salt. For food-grade sodium CMC, confirm that the product name, salt form, CAS number and additive identity agree throughout the supplier documents.

Are food grade and detergent grade CMC interchangeable?

No. The grades may differ in purity, impurities, microbiological controls, documentation and functional targets. A detergent-grade specification should not be used to qualify material for food manufacturing.

How do I select the right CMC grade for my food?

Start with the application, target texture, process temperature, pH, salt or sugar content, other hydrocolloids and desired viscosity. Our team can use that information to recommend candidates for your own pilot and stability trials.

Can I request a sample and product documents?

Samples and applicable documents can be discussed for qualified projects. Include the grade or specification, application, test plan, destination and courier details in your inquiry.

What information is needed for an accurate quotation?

Provide your intended food application, required standard, full viscosity method and range, degree of substitution if relevant, particle size, quantity, packaging and destination. Attaching your own specification sheet is the fastest route to a useful quotation.
