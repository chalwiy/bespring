Selection depends on refined carrageenan or processed Eucheuma seaweed identity, kappa, iota or lambda behavior, and the test method used to compare grades. Identity before performance

What is food grade carrageenan?

Carrageenan is a family of sulfated galactan hydrocolloids from specified red seaweeds. The 2007 JECFA carrageenan specification identifies INS 407 and CAS 9000-07-1 and lists thickening, gelling, stabilization and emulsification functions. Polymer composition, counter-ions and any standardizers affect grade selection; one fixed molecular formula, molecular weight or hydrate does not describe all commercial carrageenan.

Processed Eucheuma seaweed (PES), often called semi-refined carrageenan, is a separate INS 407a identity. It has a different processing definition and more retained cellulosic matter. It is not identified merely by a lower gel-strength value. Confirm the additive identity and full ingredient declaration for each offer; food, feed and technical grades require separate qualification.

Choose the polymer response

Kappa, iota and lambda carrageenan are not interchangeable

κ-carrageenan

Firm, short gel tendency

Potassium is particularly effective for κ gel development. Screen firmness, brittleness, syneresis, melt/set temperatures and synergy with proteins or other gums.

ι-carrageenan

Soft, elastic gel tendency

Calcium can support elastic structures in suitable iota systems. Evaluate syneresis, freeze–thaw, ion level and recovery after shear in the actual recipe; these properties are not fixed for all grades.

λ-carrageenan

Viscosity without a classic gel

Predominantly non-gelling behavior suits thickening targets. Define cold/hot process, shear curve, protein interaction and final mouthfeel.

A label such as “kappa” is still incompleteCommercial materials may contain mixed carrageenan populations and added salts or standardizers. Require the actual gel curve, ion system and application benchmark for the offered grade. FAO's carrageenan guide explains the differing ion and gel responses; they are general selection principles, not guarantees for every commercial blend.

Hydration and gel history control performance

Define the carrageenan process window

  1. 1

    Disperse without lumps

    Dry-blend with a suitable carrier or use validated pre-wetting; control shear and powder addition rate.

  2. 2

    Hydrate at the required temperature

    Refined carrageenan is typically evaluated with heating; JECFA's identity reference uses about 80°C. Exact hydration depends on type, salts and formulation.

  3. 3

    Control ions and protein

    Measure potassium, calcium, sodium, milk proteins and other charged ingredients before changing gum level.

  4. 4

    Record cooling and shear

    Gel set, pumping and filling depend on temperature history, shear during transition and final storage.

Carrageenan applications and grade selection

Dairy beverages

Measure cocoa/particle suspension, casein interaction, viscosity, heat stability, sediment and over-gelation.

Plant-based drinks

Protein source, calcium fortification, oil emulsion, homogenization and heat process can change response.

Processed meat

Test brine dispersion, cook yield, purge, slice texture, syneresis, salt/phosphate interaction and sensory quality.

Gels and desserts

Select firm versus elastic texture, opacity, flavor release, cut behavior, melt/set temperature and freeze–thaw need.

Regulatory boundary: INS identity does not authorize every category or dosage. Confirm the destination rule, finished-food category, use level and label declaration.

Approve a specification for the exact identity and grade

Request separate refined-carrageenan and PES specifications, including seaweed/process identity, predominant κ/ι/λ behavior, standardizers, analytical methods and application rheology. Match test concentration, salt composition, heating/cooling, rest time, temperature and instrument geometry before comparing gel values. Clarify the legacy g/cm² force/area convention; it is not automatically equivalent to a penetration-force measurement.

Important standard difference: the cited JECFA sulfate range is 15–40% on a dried basis, whereas 21 CFR 172.620 specifies 20–40% on a dry-weight basis for its defined carrageenan. The existing 15–40% commercial reference is retained; request a batch result and a destination-specific specification rather than assuming US conformity. Sulfate content and total ash are separate measurements.

Additional evidence: the cited JECFA INS 407 specification includes acid-insoluble matter ≤2% and residual ethanol, isopropanol and methanol ≤0.1%, singly or combined; these results are not supplied in the commercial table. Coliform absence in 5 g does not replace E. coli negative in 1 g, and Salmonella absence in 10 g needs method and sampling-plan review. Obtain the offered-grade TDS/specification, SDS, representative and batch COA, ingredient/source declarations and any required certificates; confirm availability and coverage.

Buyer questions

Food grade carrageenan FAQ

Which food grade carrageenan type should I request?

Begin with the required texture and process: kappa for firmer gel tendencies, iota for elastic gel tendencies and lambda for predominantly non-gelling thickening. Confirm the actual polymer blend, salts, standardizers and food-system trial rather than selecting by type name alone.

Are INS 407 and INS 407a the same purchasing specification?

No. JECFA defines carrageenan as INS 407 and processed Eucheuma seaweed as INS 407a, with different processing and insoluble-matter characteristics. Specify the required identity and grade; a lower gel-strength result does not itself identify PES.

Can gel-strength numbers be compared between suppliers?

Only with matching concentration, salt system, heating/cooling, rest time, measurement temperature and instrument geometry. Clarify the reporting unit and force/area convention. The historical refined and semi-refined figures on this page are not equivalent-grade evidence.

Does the reference table prove JECFA or US conformity?

No. It is a commercial screening reference and lacks some standard fields. In particular, its sulfate range starts at 15%, while the cited US carrageenan provision starts at 20% on a dry-weight basis. Review the full destination specification, methods and actual batch COA.

Does carrageenan always stabilize milk or plant-based drinks?

No fixed outcome applies. Protein, ions, solids, pH, homogenization and heat history affect the response. Use a control and grade trials to check suspension, viscosity, separation and unwanted gelation in the actual beverage.

What documents and details should a bulk RFQ contain?

Provide INS identity, kappa/iota/lambda target, ingredient declaration, application, ions/protein/solids, process temperatures and shear, rheology method, quality limits, quantity, packaging, destination and delivery terms. Request TDS, SDS, COA and required declarations or certificates with scope confirmed.
