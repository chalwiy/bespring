Product overview

What is food grade anhydrous DCP?

Food grade dicalcium phosphate anhydrous is calcium hydrogen phosphate, CaHPO4, CAS 7757-93-9 and INS/E341(ii). JECFA also uses the synonyms dibasic calcium phosphate and dicalcium phosphate.

It is sparingly soluble in water and contains no water of crystallization. Particle size, assay basis, loss on drying, impurity limits and destination-market requirements are therefore more useful purchasing variables than the abbreviation “DCP” by itself.

DCP dihydrate

  • Molar mass: 172.09 g/mol
  • JECFA loss on drying: 18–22% at 200°C for 3 hours

Actual use levels and permitted food categories depend on the formulation and destination market. Complete technical trials and regulatory review before commercial use.

Dough conditioning

JECFA lists dough conditioner as a functional use. Evaluate this grade in the intended flour system and process; this listing does not establish a specific dough-strengthening effect.

Yeast food systems

JECFA also lists yeast food. Confirm the complete nutrient system and permitted application rather than assuming a universal effect.

Mineral ingredient

For calcium and phosphorus applications, request measured nutrient content and its reporting basis. Check dispersion, sedimentation and sensory behavior in the food system; composition alone does not establish a finished-food nutrition claim.

Dry formulations

For dry blends, specify the physical form and particle-size distribution needed for your equipment. Confirm bulk density, flow and blend uniformity by grade-specific data and trials rather than assuming every DCP powder behaves alike.

Official reference and specification differences

The FAO/WHO JECFA calcium hydrogen phosphate monograph identifies CAS 7757-93-9, INS 341(ii), CaHPO4, 98–102% assay after drying, sparing water solubility, and dough-conditioner/yeast-food functions. The U.S. FDA dibasic calcium phosphate record independently lists CAS 7757-93-9 and recognized technical effects. These references support identity context; they do not certify a Bespring batch or authorize every food application.

Lead requires a tighter limit for JECFA qualification: the commercial lead value of 0.0005% equals 5 mg/kg, whereas the linked JECFA monograph specifies no more than 4 mg/kg. The listed arsenic and fluoride values convert to 3 mg/kg and 50 mg/kg respectively, matching those numerical JECFA limits. This does not establish full compliance: obtain a specification meeting every applicable requirement and batch evidence using the required methods.

JECFA specifies anhydrous loss on drying of no more than 2% at 200°C for 3 hours; the commercial ignition result does not demonstrate that requirement. “Heavy metals (as Pb)” is a separate aggregate parameter, not the individual lead test or proof that all metal limits are met.

For U.S. food-use context, see 21 CFR 182.1217 and the nutrient-supplement provisions in 21 CFR 182.8217. These provisions concern use under good manufacturing practice; assess your food category, formulation and labeling separately.

Buyer questions

Dicalcium phosphate FAQ

What is food grade anhydrous dicalcium phosphate used for?

JECFA identifies calcium hydrogen phosphate as a dough conditioner and yeast food. Anhydrous DCP may also be evaluated as a calcium/phosphorus ingredient where permitted. Suitability depends on the selected grade, food system, processing and market requirements; this page does not guarantee a leavening effect.

How does anhydrous DCP differ from DCP dihydrate?

Anhydrous DCP is CaHPO₄, CAS 7757-93-9, with a molar mass of 136.06 g/mol. DCP dihydrate is CaHPO₄·2H₂O, CAS 7789-77-7, with a molar mass of 172.09 g/mol. Quote the forms separately and compare their analytical bases and application data before selecting a grade.

Does the reference specification establish JECFA compliance?

No. The commercial lead limit is 5 mg/kg, while the cited JECFA limit is 4 mg/kg. The listed loss on ignition also does not demonstrate JECFA loss on drying. Request a signed specification addressing all applicable requirements and a batch COA with defined test methods.

Is anhydrous DCP a direct replacement for MCP or other leavening acids?

No equal-weight substitution is proposed. MCP, DCP and TCP have different identities; hydration form and grade also matter. For a baking system, qualify the leavening acid, neutralization balance and reaction behavior under your mixing, holding and heating conditions.

Does DCP dissolve readily in water?

JECFA describes calcium hydrogen phosphate as sparingly soluble in water. For a food formulation, evaluate dispersion, particle size and sedimentation under the actual pH, mixing and processing conditions rather than treating it as a readily soluble calcium ingredient.

Can feed grade DCP be ordered under this food specification?

No. This page is for food grade anhydrous DCP. Feed grade qualification uses its own nutrient guarantees, impurity limits, species requirements and documentation; a feed COA is not evidence of food-grade suitability.

What packaging and minimum order quantity are listed?

The reference packing is 25 kg per bag, with a stated minimum order quantity of 500 kg. Reference loading is 22 metric tons per 20-foot container. Confirm current order terms, palletizing and destination-specific loading before ordering.

Which qualification documents should a DCP buyer request?

Request the current signed specification, SDS and representative COA, followed by the shipment batch COA. Ask for the required food-safety, origin, allergen or religious-certification documents and confirm their availability and exact product, site and validity scope.

What information is needed for a DCP quotation?

Provide anhydrous or dihydrate identity, food standard, assay basis, critical impurity limits, particle-size requirement, intended food application, quantity, packaging, destination country or port, delivery terms and timing.
