Direct product answer

What is food grade sodium acid pyrophosphate?

Food grade sodium acid pyrophosphate (SAPP), also called disodium pyrophosphate or disodium dihydrogen diphosphate, is INS 450(i), CAS 7758-16-9 and formula Na2H2P2O7.

This page separates the supplied commercial specification from independent identity references. Legal use, dosage and documentation must be confirmed for the destination market and exact food application.

Functional roles

Why is SAPP used in food processing?

Reaction rate, neutralizing value, particle size, dosage and the complete system determine performance.

Function group 01

Raising and buffering

JECFA recognizes SAPP as a raising agent and buffer. In bakery systems it supplies controlled acidity for reaction with sodium bicarbonate.

Function group 02

Sequestration and moisture management

JECFA also lists sequestration. The supplied information identifies combined-phosphate use for moisture retention in selected meat products.

Buyer questions

Food grade SAPP FAQ

What is food grade SAPP used for?

SAPP is used as a raising agent, buffer and sequestrant, especially in qualified bakery leavening systems. The supplied sheet also identifies combined-phosphate use in cooked ham and canned meat.

Is SAPP the same as tetrasodium pyrophosphate?

No. SAPP is acidic disodium pyrophosphate, INS 450(i), with formula Na2H2P2O7. TSPP is alkaline tetrasodium pyrophosphate, INS 450(iii).

What is the reference assay?

The supplied sheet states at least 95.0%. Confirm the signed specification and batch COA.

What bakery parameters should be specified?

State reaction rate or dough rate, neutralizing value, particle size, target product and leavening system.

What are the packing and minimum order?

The reference is 25 kg per bag, 24 metric tons per 20-foot container and a 500 kg minimum order.
