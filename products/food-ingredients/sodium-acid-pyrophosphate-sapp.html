What is sodium acid pyrophosphate?

Sodium acid pyrophosphate (SAPP), also called disodium dihydrogen diphosphate, is the acidic disodium salt of diphosphoric acid: CAS 7758-16-9, Na2H2P2O7, INS 450(i) / E450(i). The JECFA specification identifies it as a water-soluble white crystalline powder or granules and lists raising, buffering and sequestrant functions. This page covers the food grade white powder described in Bespring’s reference sheet; it does not offer a separate hydrated or industrial grade.

SAPP supplies acid that reacts with sodium bicarbonate to release carbon dioxide; it is a chemical leavening ingredient, not yeast or a fermentation agent. Its acidic chemistry differs from alkaline tetrasodium pyrophosphate (TSPP). For bakery selection, compare the offered grade’s gas-release curve and acid–bicarbonate balance in the actual formula.

The defining purchase decision

Select SAPP by reaction profile—not by name alone

SAPP 15, 28 or 40 labels refer to supplier-defined reaction-rate grades, not purity percentages. Request the exact grade, method and acceptance range for the offered material; the current Bespring reference sheet does not identify a numbered grade.

01

Reaction rate or gas-release curve

Dough rate of reaction (DRR), or rate of reaction (ROR), describes gas release under a defined test. Ask for the percentage of available carbon dioxide released, elapsed time, temperature, test formula and bicarbonate grade. Compare these conditions with your mixing, holding and heating sequence.

02

Neutralizing value (NV)

NV is the mass of sodium bicarbonate neutralized by 100 mass units of leavening acid. It describes neutralizing capacity, not gas-release speed. The ARM & HAMMER leavening reference lists SAPP at 72 on a sodium bicarbonate basis; this is an industry reference, not a measured or guaranteed Bespring value. Request the offered grade’s NV, basis and method.

03

Particle-size distribution

Request the particle-size distribution, sieve method and any required bulk-density or flow data. Trial mixing uniformity, dissolution and dispersion under your process conditions; the reference sheet specifies white powder but does not define a mesh grade.

04

Finished-product validation

Keep formula, water addition, mixing, hold time and bake or fry profile controlled. Compare pH, gas retention, volume, shape, crumb, browning and sensory results against your approved benchmark. No single grade number predicts all of these outcomes.

Do not substitute on assay alone: two materials can both meet a ≥95% assay while producing different leavening behavior. Request reaction-rate, NV and particle-size data before a plant trial.

Application-specific validation

Where food grade SAPP is evaluated

SAPP is evaluated in the food applications below using a grade matched to the process. U.S. 21 CFR 182.1087 recognizes its use under good manufacturing practice. Check the applicable food-category rules and product requirements in your destination market; an INS/E identity is not a worldwide use permission.

Cakes, muffins and doughnuts

Match mixing-to-deposit time and the bake or fry profile to the grade’s measured gas-release curve. Evaluate volume, symmetry, crumb and taste; for doughnuts, include fryer conditions and oil uptake in the trial.

Biscuits, cookies and crackers

Where chemical leavening is required, compare spread, lift, surface appearance and browning under fixed process conditions. Review bicarbonate balance, finished pH and powder distribution alongside reaction timing.

Baking powder and dry mixes

Review compatibility with bicarbonate and any second leavening acid, then protect the blend from moisture that could trigger premature reaction.

Refrigerated or frozen dough

Request grade-specific data for cold holding, freezing, thawing and delayed bake-off. Reproduce that sequence in a trial; do not assume every SAPP grade provides the same holding tolerance or later gas release.

A different SAPP buying intent

Processed potato color control

The USDA 2010 technical evaluation describes SAPP as a sequestrant used to inhibit after-cooking darkening associated with iron–phenolic reactions in processed potatoes. This use targets color control, not leavening or preservation. Qualify treatment conditions and finished-food results separately from bakery DRR/ROR data; the historical report is technical context, not proof of current market permission.

  • Define the processFrench fries, flakes, granules, frozen or cooked potato products have different treatment points.
  • Control the variablesRecord potato variety, iron in raw material/process water, blanching, treatment point, concentration, contact time and final pH.
  • Measure the outcomeCompare after-cooking color, flavor, texture and phosphate-related limits against an untreated control and approved benchmark.

What JECFA independently supports

The linked JECFA monograph lists assay ≥95%, pH 3.7–5.0 for a 1-in-100 solution, loss on drying ≤0.5%, water-insoluble matter ≤1%, fluoride ≤10 mg/kg, arsenic ≤3 mg/kg and lead ≤4 mg/kg. Its assay uses a sample pre-dried at 105°C for 4 h. Matching numerical limits alone does not establish compliance.

The pH difference and additional grade data

The commercial pH interval includes results below JECFA’s 3.7 minimum. A result within 3.5–4.5 therefore does not necessarily meet JECFA’s pH requirement. Request matched-method batch evidence. Reaction-rate grade, curve, NV, P2O5, particle size, bulk density and shelf life require separate confirmation.

EU specification reference: the original 2012 publication of Regulation (EU) No 231/2012, E450(i) lists arsenic and lead at ≤1 mg/kg each, stricter than the commercial ≤3 and ≤4 mg/kg above. For an EU inquiry, request the current amended specification and matching batch evidence, including all additional required tests; this table does not establish EU conformity.

Buyer questions

Sodium acid pyrophosphate FAQ

What does a SAPP grade number mean?

A number such as 15, 28 or 40 identifies a supplier-defined reaction-rate grade, not the assay percentage. Obtain the DRR/ROR method, test formula, time, temperature and acceptance range. Bespring’s reference sheet does not confirm a numbered grade.

Is neutralizing value the same as reaction rate?

No. Neutralizing value describes the mass of sodium bicarbonate neutralized by 100 mass units of leavening acid. Reaction rate describes how quickly gas is released under stated conditions. Request both to assess acid balance and process timing.

Is SAPP the same as tetrasodium pyrophosphate?

No. SAPP is acidic disodium dihydrogen diphosphate, INS 450(i), Na₂H₂P₂O₇. Tetrasodium pyrophosphate (TSPP), INS 450(iii), is a different, alkaline phosphate. They are not direct substitutes.

Can one SAPP grade replace another in baking?

Not on assay alone. Compare reaction rate, neutralizing value, particle size and the applicable specification. Reproduce mixing, holding, water addition and bake or fry conditions in a controlled trial before changing the approved grade.

Why is SAPP used in processed potatoes?

USDA technical literature describes sequestration of iron to help limit after-cooking darkening. Confirm the treatment point, potato variety, process-water conditions, contact time and final pH, then evaluate color, flavor and texture. This is separate from bakery leavening.

Does the reference specification establish JECFA compliance?

No. The commercial pH range is 3.5–4.5; the linked JECFA monograph specifies 3.7–5.0 for a 1-in-100 solution. Confirm methods, reporting bases and batch results for all applicable requirements. A COA reports results for an identified lot against a stated specification.

Which documents and details should accompany a SAPP RFQ?

State the food application, process timing, destination standard, required DRR/ROR and method, NV, particle-size requirements, benchmark specification, quantity, packaging, destination port and delivery terms. Request the current specification, TDS, SDS, representative or lot-specific COA and any required product/site certificates; availability and scope must be confirmed.
