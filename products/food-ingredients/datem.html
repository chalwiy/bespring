Commercial distribution, not a pure molecule

What is DATEM E472e?

JECFA defines DATEM as mixed glycerol esters of mono- and diacetyltartaric acid and fatty acids from edible fats and oils. Reaction conditions and raw-material proportions affect the component distribution; commercial material can also contain small amounts of free glycerol, fatty acids, tartaric acid and acetic acid.

The accepted identity is therefore the complete DATEM/INS 472e definition plus a source-specific specification. CAS alone cannot establish composition or bakery equivalence.

Strengthen the dough interface

How DATEM changes yeast-leavened dough

DATEM is commonly screened as an anionic dough-strengthening emulsifier. Its practical value is determined by interactions among flour proteins, starch, lipids, water, mixing energy and fermentation—not by a generic “emulsification” claim.

01

Mixing development

Track water absorption, development time, stability, resistance and overmix tolerance under controlled mixer energy.

02

Gas-cell stability

Measure dough extensibility, resistance, fermentation expansion and collapse rather than assuming more strength is always better.

03

Oven response

Compare oven spring, loaf volume, sidewall shape, break/shred and crumb-cell distribution.

04

Eating and shelf life

Check crumb firmness, resilience, chew, dryness and staling; excess strengthening can harm machinability or texture.

Define the required strength windowWeak flour may need more reinforcement; already strong or highly oxidized dough can become too elastic and insufficiently extensible. Optimize with the entire improver system.

DATEM bakery qualification protocol

Lock raw materials

Use one flour lot and record protein, damaged starch, absorption, ascorbic acid, enzymes, mono-/diglycerides and other improvers.

Run a dose matrix

Include untreated control, current benchmark and multiple candidate levels. Do not publish a universal dosage.

Stress the process

Test normal and extended mixing, proof time and temperature, line rest and process interruptions.

Measure outcomes

Record farinograph/mix data where available, dough handling, proof height, specific volume, crumb image, texture and shelf life.

Performance boundary: DATEM can support gas retention and dough tolerance in a suitable system, but this page makes no loaf-volume, yield or shelf-life guarantee.

Select by mechanism and label

DATEM vs. adjacent dough improvers

DATEM

Anionic emulsifier commonly selected for dough strengthening and gas-cell stability.

SSL / CSL

Lactylates have different chemistry and performance. Compare strength, crumb, permitted use and label declaration.

Mono- & diglycerides

Often screened more for crumb softness and starch interaction; they are not direct DATEM equivalents.

Enzyme systems

Enzymes act catalytically and depend on substrate/process. Compare flour variability, tolerance, shelf life and label strategy.

Buyer questions

DATEM E472e FAQ

Is DATEM a single chemical compound?

No. It is a distribution of mixed glycerol esters whose composition depends on raw materials and processing. Use INS 472e plus the source specification.

Does every DATEM grade perform the same in bread?

No. Composition, fatty-acid source, physical form, dispersibility and flour/process interactions can change performance.

Is more DATEM always better for loaf volume?

No. Excess strengthening can reduce extensibility or harm handling and texture. Optimize with a control and dose matrix.

Can DATEM be replaced one-for-one with SSL or monoglycerides?

Not reliably. Their chemistry, mechanism, legal uses and effects on strength versus softness differ. Reformulate and validate.

What should a bulk DATEM RFQ contain?

State application, flour/process, benchmark, target bake metrics, fatty-acid source restrictions, form, quantity, packing, destination and documents.
