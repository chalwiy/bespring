Defined molecule, variable commercial profile

What is food grade vanillin?

Vanillin is the defined flavoring substance 4-hydroxy-3-methoxybenzaldehyde. JECFA lists CAS 121-33-5, molecular weight 152.15 and a sweet, creamy, vanilla odor. It is slightly soluble in water and freely soluble in ethanol.

Matching the molecule does not by itself match the grade. Origin route, trace volatiles, color, crystal form, particle behavior and storage history can change the sensory and processing result.

Vanillin, ethyl vanillin and vanilla are not synonyms

Defined substance

Vanillin

CAS 121-33-5. Approve its identity, route, purity and sensory fingerprint for the intended label and formula.

Different molecule

Ethyl vanillin

A separate flavoring substance with a different CAS, formula and intensity profile. It is not a stronger grade of the same identity.

Flavoring preparation

Vanilla extract

A multi-component preparation from vanilla material, subject to distinct composition, standard-of-identity and labeling rules.

Origin claim

“Natural” vanillin

A chemistry name does not prove natural status. Obtain process, feedstock, traceability and destination-specific claim review.

EU natural-label boundaryRegulation (EC) 1334/2008 defines a natural flavoring substance by permitted physical, enzymatic or microbiological production from qualifying source material. A “natural vanilla” reference has additional source-percentage rules. Confirm the current legal description before artwork approval.

01

Neat odor screen

Compare sweet/creamy vanilla character and reject smoky, phenolic, medicinal, woody, chemical or oxidized off-notes against a retained control.

02

Standard solution

Fix solvent, concentration, temperature, equilibration time, vessel and blind coding so source comparisons are repeatable.

03

Run multiple levels in the actual base. Track threshold, balance, aftertaste and interactions with cocoa, dairy fat, sweetener, salt and other aromas.

Shelf-life check

Monitor aroma retention, color, package interaction and off-note development after realistic heat, oxygen, light and storage exposure.

Vanillin application and addition-point guide

Chocolate & confectionery

Evaluate cocoa balance, fat-phase distribution, refining/conching exposure, bitterness masking, sweetness and aroma retention.

Bakery & biscuits

Compare dough distribution, bake loss, crust/crumb aroma, color interaction and packaged shelf-life—not raw dough aroma alone.

Dairy & frozen desserts

Test fat/protein partitioning, pasteurization, aging, overrun, cold-temperature flavor release and aftertaste.

Beverages & dry mixes

Validate premixing or solvent/carrier strategy, clarity or sediment, pH, packaging, reconstitution and aroma loss.

Dosage boundary: There is no universal use level. Flavor intensity depends on source profile, matrix, process, serving temperature and other aromas; legality and label declaration also depend on the destination.

Buyer questions

Food grade vanillin FAQ

Is vanillin the same as vanilla extract?

No. Vanillin is one defined molecule. Vanilla extract is a multi-component preparation with separate composition and labeling requirements.

Is vanillin the same as ethyl vanillin?

No. They are different chemical and flavoring identities. Do not substitute solely on a claimed intensity ratio; reformulate and validate sensory performance.

Can CAS 121-33-5 prove that vanillin is natural?

No. CAS confirms molecular identity, not feedstock or manufacturing route. A natural claim requires source/process evidence and destination-specific legal review.

How should vanillin be added to a food?

Choose a permitted solvent, carrier, premix or phase based on solubility and the matrix. Validate addition point, mixing and heat exposure in the actual process.

What should a bulk vanillin RFQ include?

State vanillin identity, source/claim requirement, benchmark, application, process, assay/impurity needs, sensory protocol, quantity, packing, destination and certificates.
