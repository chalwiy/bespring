Product identity

What is pea protein?

Pea protein is the protein-rich fraction obtained from peas after separating portions of starch, fiber and other components. Commercial ingredients differ with the raw pea, wet or dry fractionation, extraction pH, heat history, drying and any subsequent modification.

It is a food ingredient rather than a single chemical additive and has no universal CAS, INS number or composition. “Pea protein” therefore cannot serve as a complete product specification. Buyers must identify the grade, ingredient list, manufacturing route, protein basis and intended function.

Define the product before comparing it

Pea protein isolate, concentrate and functional grades

Names and minimum protein thresholds are not globally interchangeable. Do not infer a grade from marketing terminology alone; use the offered specification and destination-market naming rules.

Grade 01

Pea protein isolate

A more highly purified protein fraction. Ask for protein on both as-is and dry basis, residual starch/fiber/fat, ash and the process used to obtain the isolate.

Grade 02

Pea protein concentrate

Generally retains more non-protein pea components. Confirm actual composition and whether flavor, color, water binding or nutrition is the primary goal.

Grade 03

Functional / modified grade

May be designed for dispersibility, solubility, emulsification, gelation or sensory performance. Declare processing aids and modification where required.

Grade 04

Hydrolyzed or textured form

Hydrolysis changes solubility, viscosity and bitterness; extrusion creates structured pieces with separate hydration and texture specifications.

Protein result must be reproducibleState the analytical method, measured nitrogen, nitrogen-to-protein conversion factor, dry or as-is basis, and moisture method. Two “80% protein” offers are not comparable if their methods or reporting bases differ.

Application-led sourcing

Select pea protein by finished-product behavior

A high protein assay alone does not establish suitability. Use the actual formula, water quality, pH, shear, heat process and storage conditions.

Ready-to-drink beverages

Measure wetting, dispersion, solubility, viscosity, sediment, heat stability, color and flavor at the target pH and protein load.

Dry nutrition powders

Evaluate instantization, dust, scoop density, reconstitution, foam, grit, flavor release and stability after package opening.

Plant-based meat

Test hydration, fat binding, emulsification, gel strength, extrusion response, bite, cooking loss and reheating stability.

Bakery, pasta & snacks

Track dough water demand, machinability, volume, crumb or bite, browning, flavor and declared protein after processing.

Function and sensory are method-dependent

Build a performance fingerprint for each pea protein grade

Hydration

Wetting, dispersion & solubility

Record water temperature, pH, solids, mixing energy, hydration time and centrifugation or filtration method. “Soluble” without conditions is not comparable.

Interface

Emulsion & foam

Distinguish capacity from stability and test the intended oil phase, ionic strength, homogenization and heat process.

Structure

Viscosity, gel & water holding

Measure concentration, heat/cool cycle, shear, yield stress, gel strength, syneresis and cooking loss as applicable.

Sensory

Color, grit & off-notes

Use a defined panel and reference standard for beany, green, bitter or astringent notes; record masking and flavor interactions.

Why pH belongs in every comparisonProtein charge and aggregation change with pH. A grade that disperses at neutral pH may form haze, sediment or complexes in an acid beverage. Test across the product's real pH and mineral environment, including shelf-life drift.

Avoid unsupported nutrition shortcuts

Do not describe a grade as a “complete protein,” assign an amino-acid score, digestibility value or health benefit without grade-specific analytical data, the required scoring method and destination-market claim review. Protein quantity and protein quality are separate questions.

Allergen and claim control

Pea protein is not automatically “allergen-free”

Pea declaration

Identify pea protein clearly in the ingredient list according to destination rules. “Not a priority allergen” in one country does not mean zero reaction risk.

Peanut cross-reactivity

Health Canada notes that some people with peanut allergy may react to pea protein. Avoid blanket hypoallergenic or allergen-free claims.

Cross-contact

Qualify shared lines, cleaning validation and the complete allergen statement for the exact manufacturing site.

GMO, vegan & origin

Define each claim, required evidence, testing or chain-of-custody scope and change-control period before publication.

Buyer questions

Pea protein FAQ

What is the difference between pea protein isolate and concentrate?

They differ in the degree of protein enrichment and retained non-protein components, but naming thresholds can vary. Compare actual composition, analytical basis, process and application data rather than the name alone.

What protein percentage does Bespring's pea protein contain?

No source-specific value is published on this page. Request the current grade specification with method, nitrogen conversion factor, moisture method and dry/as-is basis.

Is pea protein soluble in water?

Performance ranges from dispersible suspensions to more soluble functional grades. Result depends on process history, pH, minerals, concentration, temperature, mixing and the measurement method.

How should pea protein be tested in beverages?

At the intended protein load, measure wetting, dispersion, viscosity, particle size or grit, sediment, heat stability, flavor and shelf-life behavior across the real pH and mineral environment.

Is pea protein allergen-free?

No blanket allergen-free claim should be made. Pea can cause allergic reactions, and Health Canada notes possible reactions among some peanut-allergic people. Review local rules and source cross-contact controls.

What should a pea protein RFQ include?

Provide grade type, target protein and method, application, pH and heat process, critical functional and sensory limits, quantity, packing, destination, claims and documents.
