What is food grade guar gum?

Guar gum—guar flour or gum cyamopsis—is produced primarily from the endosperm of Cyamopsis tetragonolobus seeds. The cited JECFA specification identifies CAS 9000-30-0 and INS 412 and describes a high-molecular-weight galactomannan with an approximate mannose-to-galactose ratio of 2:1.

It is a variable natural polymer, not a single small molecule with a meaningful fixed molecular formula. It has a molecular-weight distribution rather than one molecular weight or defined hydrate. Grade selection depends on purity, particle size, hydration rate, viscosity curve and microbiology. Native food-grade guar, partially hydrolyzed guar, chemically modified guar and formulated gum blends require separate identity and performance review; industrial grades are not food-grade substitutes.

Viscosity develops over time

Control guar gum dispersion and hydration

Finer powder can hydrate faster but also forms surface-wetted lumps more readily. A final two-hour viscosity alone does not reveal production performance.

01

Particle distribution

Specify sieve profile, not only a nominal mesh. Particle distribution can change wetting and hydration time. Verify the curve in the proposed method rather than treating mesh alone as a rate guarantee.

02

Water availability

Sugar, salts, proteins and other water-binding solids can slow hydration. Establish the order of addition in the complete formula.

03

Mixing energy

Dry blend with a suitable carrier or use validated wetting. Record mixer type, speed, addition rate and batch geometry to prevent fish-eyes.

04

Time and temperature

Track viscosity at defined intervals and temperature. Faster hydration with heat does not guarantee the same final viscosity after prolonged thermal processing.

A comparable viscosity needs the entire methodState gum concentration and dry/as-is basis, water quality, temperature, dispersion procedure, hydration times, pH, Brookfield model, spindle, rpm, sample temperature and reporting unit.

Application-specific process targets

Guar gum applications and grade selection

Sauces & dressings

Measure pour curve, suspension, cling, pumpability and acid/salt stability across shelf life.

Bakery & fillings

Evaluate water distribution, dough handling, bake loss, softness, filling flow and staling—not “moisture retention” alone.

Frozen desserts

Test mix aging, overrun, meltdown, iciness, heat shock and interaction with proteins and other stabilizers.

Dry beverage mixes

Control segregation, dust, dispersibility, lumping, early viscosity and final mouthfeel under consumer mixing conditions.

Regulatory boundary: Confirm food category, level, label and any origin-specific import controls. 21 CFR 184.1339 lists US identity/purity requirements, functions and category-specific maximum levels on a food-as-served basis. These are legal use conditions, not universal formulation recommendations.

Approve the specification, then review the batch COA

These commercial values do not establish full JECFA conformity. The protein reference is stricter than the cited JECFA value, while coliform counts cannot stand in for its E. coli test. Ask for the missing microorganism and residual-solvent results and explicit borate/starch methods and acceptance criteria. Sodium-borate gel formation is a laboratory identity test; it is separate from the borate purity test and is not a food formulation instruction.

Functional grade: the ≥5,000 mPa·s reference at 1% / 2 h needs concentration basis, water, temperature, mixing procedure and instrument settings. Specify the required early viscosity as well as final viscosity. mPa·s and cP have equal numerical values, but changing test conditions prevents a meaningful comparison.

Document package: request the offered-grade specification/TDS, SDS, representative and batch COA, botanical origin, ingredient/allergen declarations and any required certificate with its site, product and validity scope. Confirm availability before approval. Agree particle distribution, packaging and liner, storage, shelf life and delivery terms; the page provides no verified storage temperature or shelf-life duration.

Choose by rheology and process

Guar gum vs. xanthan, carrageenan and konjac

Guar gum

Rapid cold-water viscosity; hydration rate and lump prevention are key.

Xanthan gum

Compare pseudoplastic suspension, yield behavior, acid/salt tolerance and mouthfeel.

Carrageenan

Compare κ/ι/λ gel or protein interaction; not a direct viscosity substitution.

Konjac gum

Compare glucomannan hydration, alkaline gelation and synergy in defined blends.

These links support function and process comparisons, not equal-dose substitution. For dairy formulation context, review the dairy stabilizer and emulsifying-salt guide; a thickener and an emulsifying salt perform different jobs.

Buyer questions

Food grade guar gum FAQ

What defines a fast-hydrating food grade guar gum?

A time-to-viscosity curve under a specified test method. Particle size, dispersion, water quality, temperature and formulation solids all influence hydration. A mesh label or two-hour final viscosity alone does not establish performance on a short production cycle.

Can two ≥5,000 mPa·s guar grades be treated as equivalent?

Compare concentration and mass basis, water, temperature, hydration procedure/time, instrument, spindle and speed first. The existing 1% / 2 h reference is incomplete without these details; also compare early viscosity and trials in the real food.

Why does guar gum form lumps?

Fast surface hydration can trap dry powder inside a lump. Evaluate controlled powder addition, sufficient dispersion, suitable dry pre-blending and the actual mixing equipment before selecting a faster-hydrating grade.

Does the displayed commercial specification prove JECFA conformity?

No. Some values align with the cited monograph, but test wording and missing microbiological/residual-solvent evidence need review. A batch COA must be checked against the agreed grade specification and complete methods.

Is native guar gum the same as partially hydrolyzed or modified guar?

No. Hydrolysis, chemical modification and blending can change identity, viscosity and intended use. Specify native food-grade E412 for this page and independently qualify any alternative; do not substitute industrial or personal-care grades.

What documents and information should a bulk RFQ include?

Provide food standard, application, full viscosity method and hydration target, particle distribution, formulation/process, microbiological requirements, quantity, packaging, origin restrictions, destination and delivery terms. Request TDS, SDS, COA and relevant declarations or certificates, with availability and scope confirmed.
