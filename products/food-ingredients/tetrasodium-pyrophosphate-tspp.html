Exact product identity

What is anhydrous food grade TSPP?

Tetrasodium pyrophosphate (TSPP), also called tetrasodium diphosphate or sodium pyrophosphate, is the alkaline condensed phosphate Na4P2O7. JECFA identifies CAS 7722-88-5 and INS 450(iii), with emulsifier, buffering, emulsifying-agent and sequestrant functions.

This URL centers on the supplied anhydrous food grade. JECFA also recognizes Na4P2O7·10H2O; its formula weight and ignition loss differ materially. Hydration form must match the RFQ, specification, label and COA.

Beyond a generic “water retention” claim

How TSPP changes protein and mineral systems

TSPP contributes alkalinity, ionic strength and pyrophosphate anions. Results arise from the complete formula and process.

01

pH and protein charge

Moving pH away from a protein's isoelectric region can support water association. Measure raw and finished pH; excess alkalinity can harm flavor, color or texture.

02

Protein extraction

In meat or poultry, evaluate TSPP with salt, water, temperature and mechanical action for salt-soluble protein extraction and matrix formation.

03

Ion sequestration

Pyrophosphate can complex selected polyvalent ions. Effects depend on calcium/magnesium load, proteins and competing ingredients.

04

Dissolution and sequence

Concentration, particle form, water temperature and addition order affect dissolution and local pH. Validate the real brine or blend.

Maximum uptake is not the objectiveOptimize permitted use, uniform pickup, cook yield, purge, texture and sensory quality together. More phosphate is not automatically better.

Application-specific qualification

Where food grade TSPP may be evaluated

These are screening contexts—not universal approvals, dosages or guarantees.

Meat & poultry

Target: protein extraction, bind and cook yield.

Validate: salt, pH, temperature, mix/tumble time, cook loss, purge, bite and flavor.

Seafood systems

Target: controlled moisture and texture.

Validate: species, treatment, pickup, thaw drip, sodium, appearance and sensory effects.

Processed cheese

Target: mineral/protein balance in an emulsifying-salt system.

Validate: calcium, moisture, pH, heat/shear, melt, oiling-off and set.

Mineral-sensitive liquids

Target: buffering or sequestration where permitted.

Validate: hardness, clarity, sediment, flavor, pH drift and protein/acid interaction.

Regulatory boundary: E/INS identity does not authorize every food or dosage. Confirm destination food category, maximum level, labeling and total phosphate.

Avoid acronym and function confusion

TSPP vs. STPP, TKPP and SAPP

They differ in cation, phosphate structure, acid/base character and intended function; they are not equal-weight substitutes.

Buyer questions

Food grade TSPP FAQ

Is this TSPP anhydrous or decahydrate?

This page describes anhydrous Na₄P₂O₇. The decahydrate has different formula weight and ignition loss; state the form on all documents.

Does the specification comply with JECFA?

Not as written: fluoride is 50 mg/kg versus JECFA's 10 mg/kg maximum, and several methods are missing.

Why evaluate TSPP in meat systems?

Alkalinity, ionic strength and pyrophosphate functionality can affect protein extraction and moisture management, subject to salt, temperature, process and formula.

Is it interchangeable with STPP or TKPP?

No. Compare structure, cation contribution, pH, solubility, legal identity and trial results.

What belongs in an RFQ?

State hydration form, market, application, target standard, critical limits, process, quantity, packing, destination, Incoterm and documents.
