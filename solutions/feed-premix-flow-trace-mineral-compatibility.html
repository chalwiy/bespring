Decision guide

Control both demixing and chemical contact

Order requirementsGrade, COA, TDS, SDS and logistics

A premix can pass mixer-uniformity testing and still fail after discharge, conveying, bagging or humid storage.

Physical risk comes from mismatched particle size, shape and density. Chemical risk comes from moisture, hygroscopic carriers and concentrated contact between trace minerals, vitamins, choline, acids and other reactive components.

Changing a mineral salt or amino-acid grade can alter flow even when its assay is equivalent. A finer powder may improve initial distribution yet increase dust, adhesion, caking or reaction surface.

Evidence path: characterize each major component and carrier, sample at mixer discharge and after realistic transfer, challenge packaging and storage, then recheck both uniformity and sensitive nutrient retention.

Application diagnosis

Define the premix and the handling route

Document the physical route and storage conditions alongside the ingredient composition.

01

Particles and carrier

Record the particle-size distribution, bulk density and form of the carrier and ingredients most likely to separate.

02

Mixing and transfer

Note batch fill, addition sequence, mixer condition, discharge points, conveying steps and bagging. Identify where representative samples can be collected.

03

Moisture and packaging

Identify hygroscopic ingredients, expected storage conditions, packaging and the storage period the finished premix needs to meet.

04

Sensitive components

List the vitamin or other active that needs retention monitoring. Record mineral form, coating or protection and which ingredients share the same premix.

Application-specific approval

Distinguish mixing problems from later changes

Use matched control and candidate batches to compare the mixer, the transfer line and storage separately.

01

Check the mixer

Sample under the site's mixer-validation procedure. Use a suitable approved marker with a reliable assay and record the operating conditions.

02

Check transfer and bagging

Repeat comparable sampling after the relevant transfer or bagging points. A marked difference can help identify where demixing occurs.

03

Check storage and retention

Use the intended container and realistic storage conditions. Record caking and flow, and use time-point assays for a sensitive nutrient when retention matters.

04

Record the accepted source

Include ingredient form, particle profile, marker, sampling plan and packing in change control. Re-evaluate changes that could affect uniformity or retention.

Performance evaluation

Compare three separate outcomes

Specify acceptance criteria before the trial. An acceptable mixer result alone does not validate handling or storage.

Uniformity at discharge

Record the marker method, sampling points, individual results and coefficient of variation (CV). Use the site's acceptance criterion for the intended premix.

Behavior after transfer

Compare dispersion and variation after packing or conveying. Record dust, bridging or visible separation alongside the sampling data.

Condition after storage

Record any caking and changes in flow, packaging condition and, where needed, assay of the sensitive nutrient at agreed time points.

Prepare your application inquiry

Prepare a premix ingredient inquiry

Describe where the problem appears so a carrier, physical grade or mineral source can be evaluated against the right failure.

Baseline and issue

Identify the current carrier, relevant minerals and sensitive actives, and whether variation appears after mixing, transfer or storage.

Physical targets

Include required particle distribution, bulk density and moisture basis. Identify any flow or dust limits used by the plant.

Documents and retention

Request the exact chemical form, assay methods and representative grade data. Specify any compatibility or storage study you need for the complete premix.

Trial quantities and packing

List sample and pilot quantities, expected annual demand, destination, packaging and storage requirements.

Common questions

Application and purchasing FAQ

Why does ingredient compatibility matter in feed premixes?

Concentrated minerals, vitamins, acids and hygroscopic nutrients may react or segregate. Particle engineering, carriers, packaging and storage time must be evaluated with the actual premix.

What causes segregation in a feed premix?

Large differences in particle size, shape and density, vibration during handling and an unsuitable carrier can separate ingredients. Sampling after realistic transfer is more informative than mixer-exit data alone.

Can this page provide a final formula or dosage?

No. It defines a technically relevant shortlist and evidence plan. Final use level and approval require the exact grade, actual process data, qualified technical review and applicable local rules.
