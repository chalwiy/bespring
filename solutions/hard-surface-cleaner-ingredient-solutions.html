Start with the soil and the surface—not a “stronger cleaner” claim

A kitchen degreaser, bathroom scale remover, glass cleaner and coated-floor cleaner should not share an assumed ingredient system.

Identify the soil first: triglyceride or petroleum oil, particulate matter, soap scum, carbonate scale and dried food respond to different mechanisms. Then set the substrate limits for stone, aluminum, coatings, seals, glass and plastics.

Surfactants improve wetting and suspend loosened soil; solvents assist with compatible organic soils; builders or chelants manage hard-water ions; acids or alkalis address specific deposits. Low residue, controlled evaporation and the wiping or rinsing method govern streaking as much as detergency.

Antimicrobial claims are a separate regulated decision. The presence of a quaternary ammonium compound does not by itself establish a disinfectant claim.

Claim boundaries

Define the use condition before screening ingredients

A “multi-surface” brief is incomplete until the soils, materials and cleaning method are named.

01

Soil panel

Separate grease, particulate soil, soap scum, mineral scale and dried food; include realistic aging and loading.

02

Surface panel

List glass, stone, grout, metal, coatings, plastics and seals actually covered by the label claim.

03

Application method

Fix dilution, spray or wipe delivery, dwell, mechanical action, rinse expectation and drying conditions.

04

Safety and market

Set pH, VOC, flammability, worker exposure, ingredient disclosure and any antimicrobial-claim boundaries for the destination.

RFQ built for this application

Ask suppliers questions that affect the trial

For hard surface cleaner ingredients for streak free cleaning, a useful inquiry must explain the failure mechanism and intended evidence—not only request a price per tonne.

Current condition

Fast soil removal can still be unacceptable if the product streaks, dulls a coating or attacks stone, aluminum or seals. Provide the baseline values and representative sample information.

Decision and acceptance

State how wetting, solvent power, pH and residue affect the soil and named substrate, together with the test method, mandatory limit and desired improvement.

Exact supply controls

Request identity, grade, assay, critical impurities, physical form, specification, recent COA, TDS, SDS and relevant declarations.

Trial and delivery

Provide sample and pilot quantity, annual demand, packing, destination, Incoterm, delivery window and destination-market requirements.

Formulator questions

Hard-surface cleaner FAQ

What causes streaks on glass and glossy surfaces?

Residual surfactant, salts, polymers, soil, hard-water minerals and an unsuitable evaporation or wiping profile can all contribute. Test the complete use dilution on the claimed surface.

Can one cleaner safely claim every hard surface?

No. Carbonate stone, aluminum, coatings, wood, plastics and seals tolerate different pH, solvents and contact times. Claims need individual compatibility evidence.

Does benzalkonium chloride automatically make a cleaner a disinfectant?

No. Antimicrobial claims depend on the complete registered formulation, concentration, contact time, test data, label and destination-market rules.
